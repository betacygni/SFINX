The SFINX Team

The SFINX (Star Formation IN the Galaxy) project is a joint effort between two neighboring centers in Barcelona, the Institut de Ciències de l'Espai (ICE-CSIC) and the Universitat de Barcelona (UB). Their complementary expertise across wavelengths, spatial scales and evolutionary stages is needed to build a complete picture of star formation in our Galaxy. Combining large- and small-scale studies, with special focus on magnetic fields, SFINX addresses three fundamental questions: (1) what mechanisms drive the formation and evolution of stellar clusters? (2) what is the link between accretion and feedback?; and (3) what role do magnetic fields play throughout the star formation process?

Research

To address the ambitious aims of the SFINX project, our research adopts a comprehensive multi-scale and multi-wavelength approach. By combining large-scale statistical data with high-resolution observations of individual systems, we bridge processes operating across diverse spatial scales and evolutionary stages, a framework illustrated in Figure 1 that requires the coordinated expertise of our multidisciplinary team. The project's research is structured around three primary Science Goals (SGs, see Table 1):
(SG 1) Formation and evolution of stellar clusters: Investigating how stellar clusters form and evolve over time, tracking their transition from large fragmenting clouds to open stellar associations.
(SG 2) Accretion and feedback in young stellar objects: Examining how stars gather mass through accretion, while evaluating their feedback on surrounding environments via energy release, outflows, and explosive events.
(SG 3) The polarized star formation: Exploring the critical role of magnetic fields and the physical properties of dust across all relevant scales, from massive clouds down to compact disks around stars.
